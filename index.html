About

Great systems are built by great teams — I've spent my career on both.

I started as a developer who cared a little too much about clean architecture. Somewhere along the way — probably around the time I was untangling my first distributed system in production — I realised the hardest problems weren't technical, they were about how people made decisions together. That shift didn't pull me away from engineering; it made me better at it.

Today I lead engineering squads end-to-end: from the first messy conversation about what we're actually trying to solve, through system design, to the moment something ships and someone uses it. I still read pull requests. I still get into the weeds on architecture when it matters. But my real job is making sure the team doesn't need me in the room to make good decisions.

I think in systems — how components talk to each other, how data moves, where things will break at 10x the current load — and I try to instil that same instinct in the engineers I work with, rather than being the one who always spots it first.

The Many Hats

"Engineering Lead" is what's on the org chart. In practice, the actual role shifts with what the team is missing — sometimes I'm deep in architecture, sometimes I'm running standups because the Scrum Master seat is empty, sometimes I'm the closest thing the team has to a Product Owner that week, and often I'm doing the people-management work that usually sits with an Engineering Manager. I've learned to move between these without waiting for permission or a title change.
